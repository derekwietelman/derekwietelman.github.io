        My research career began late in my undergraduate studies at [Oklahoma State University](https://okstate.edu), when I realized that, as a statistics major, I was more interested in analyzing the causal effects of public policy than I was in becoming a polling expert à la Nate Silver. My earliest interests were in understanding how land managers and firms in energy-intensive industries respond to environmental regulations. This theme is present throughout my early writings, including publications on [property value impacts of Endangered Species Act listings](/publications/chicken/), [voluntary third-party certifications for oil and gas projects](/publications/fracking/), and [energy efficiency rules for heavy-duty trucking firms](/publications/trucks/).

        ![Rapids and rocky banks of the Potomac River at Great Falls](bio/great-falls.jpg "A view of the Potomac River at Great Falls, one of my favorite places to hike in the DMV.")

        Early-career stints at [Indiana University](https://www.iu.edu) and [Resources for the Future](https://www.rff.org) further convinced me that a research career in environmental economics was the right path for me. In 2020, I started my doctoral training in the [Department of Agricultural and Resource Economics](https://arec.umd.edu) at the [University of Maryland](https://umd.edu), where I was co-advised by [David Newburn](https://sites.google.com/umd.edu/dnewburn) and [Louis Preonas](https://louispreonas.com). After graduating with my Ph.D. in 2026, I began my current position as a postdoc at the [University of Colorado Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu).

        ![Two elk standing on a paved trail beside a wooden fence, with autumn aspens behind](bio/lake-estes-elk.jpg "Two Rocky Mountain elk join me for an autumn run around Lake Estes.")

        I currently live in the Denver metro area with my wife, four-year-old daughter, and two cats. I enjoy reading, running, baseball, and searching for kid-friendly hikes along the Front Range. I’m always happy to talk about research, potential collaborations, or good trail recommendations, so please [feel free to reach out](/contact/).
